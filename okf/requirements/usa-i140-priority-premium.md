# I-140 Priority Date and Premium Processing

## Priority-date basis

- DOL-certified PERM cases: use the date DOL accepted the labor certification for processing as the petition's priority-date basis.
- I-140 categories without a DOL-certified labor certification, including EB-1 and NIW, generally use the properly filed I-140 date.
- Schedule A cases use the direct-to-USCIS labor-certification procedure and do not inherit a DOL PERM filing date.

## Earlier priority-date retention

A beneficiary with an earlier approved EB-1/EB-2/EB-3 petition may be able to retain the earlier date on a later qualifying EB-1/EB-2/EB-3 petition. Do not mark retention resolved when the prior approval was revoked/invalidated for fraud or willful misrepresentation, labor-certification revocation/invalidation, material error, or another regulatory disqualifier.

Priority-date retention does not transfer the earlier petition's substantive eligibility to the new petition.

## Premium processing

- E11, E12, E21 non-NIW, E31, E32 and EW3: current I-907 instructions list a 15-day premium-processing period.
- E13 and E21 NIW: current I-907 instructions list a 45-day period after prerequisites are met.
- Schedule A uses the premium period of its underlying eligible I-140 classification.
- Premium processing does not waive missing initial evidence, ability-to-pay, labor-certification, or beneficiary-qualification requirements.
