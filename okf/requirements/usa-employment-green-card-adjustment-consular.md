# Adjustment vs Consular Processing

## Adjustment of status — USCIS

Before a new employment-based I-485 package is treated as filing-ready:

1. applicant is physically present in the United States and the underlying employment/self-petition basis remains valid;
2. exact preference and country of chargeability are known;
3. priority date is known and compared with the correct monthly Visa Bulletin row;
4. the **USCIS-selected filing chart for that month** is verified — do not choose Dates for Filing merely because it is more favorable;
5. current I-485 filing instructions/edition/evidence are used;
6. the required Form I-693 medical package is ready under current filing instructions;
7. Supplement J is included when required for a job-offer-based case and not added blindly to EB-1A/NIW cases;
8. any requested I-765 or I-131 is treated as a separate benefit with its own eligibility/fee/travel rules.

If a status-history, unauthorized-employment, inadmissibility, removal, J-1 or comparable issue exists, return `NEEDS_AUTHORITATIVE_CONFIRMATION` rather than assuming adjustment eligibility.

## Pending employment-based I-485

Visa retrogression does not make a properly filed I-485 disappear, but Final Action availability remains necessary for approval. For a job-offer-based case involving a job change, evaluate INA 204(j) portability and Supplement J only after the current requirements — including the 180-day pending-I-485 threshold — are satisfied.

## Consular processing — Department of State / NVC

- use the approved category-specific immigrant petition and NVC/CEAC route rather than Form I-485;
- wait for case creation/instructions rather than starting DS-260 solely from the USCIS approval notice;
- use the Dates for Filing/NVC document stage and Final Action/visa issuance gate as separate concepts;
- complete fees, DS-260, civil documents, medical examination and interview preparation when NVC/consular instructions authorize the case to proceed.
